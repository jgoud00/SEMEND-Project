# DSP pipeline

Default acquisition is 4 kHz, with 2 kHz Nyquist. Alternative 2/8 kHz configurations regenerate all time-domain data at that rate. The clean carrier is seeded Gaussian activity filtered approximately 20–450 Hz and normalized to keep it within a 5 mVpp full-scale assumption. Burst envelope and intensity set clean activation. Artifacts include 50 Hz mains, 3 Hz motion, white/colored noise and an electrode DC offset. Fatigue blends toward lower-frequency activity over the trial; it is illustrative rather than physiologically validated.

After analog conditioning and 12-bit quantization, the ADC output is centered at 1.65 V and divided by nominal total gain. Thus features are **input-referred** and include effects of clipping, analog attenuation and quantization. They are not ground-truth clean EMG. Charts label volts/millivolts explicitly.

`dsp.spectrum` subtracts the mean, applies a Hann window, then computes rFFT with coherent-gain amplitude correction and single-sided endpoint correction. Welch PSD uses up to 1024 samples per segment, SciPy's default overlap and density units V²/Hz. FFT and PSD use the full trial. The dashboard's detailed waveform uses the first 250 ms without decimation; exports retain all samples.

`dsp.features` computes RMS, mean absolute value, waveform length, zero crossings, slope sign changes, power-weighted mean frequency and cumulative-power median frequency. Crossings use a 10 µV difference threshold. Welch features use up to 256 samples, so reported frequency resolution depends on acquisition rate and window length. Empty spectral power yields zero frequency features. Windowing uses 200 ms and 100 ms hop, with incomplete final windows dropped. Whole-trial RMS and windowed RMS are both returned.

The analog implementation is separate from the digital FIR decimator. `dsp.decimator` designs an odd-length Kaiser FIR with a 65 dB design margin and 450 Hz midpoint cutoff. It measures its response on 65,536 frequency bins and asserts passband ripple ≤0.1 dB through 400 Hz and stopband attenuation ≥60 dB from 500 Hz. `resample_poly` performs delay-compensated offline downsampling from 2/4/8 kHz to 1 kHz using those taps. Tests preserve a 125 Hz tone and reject a 700 Hz tone that would otherwise alias to 300 Hz. Output length/rate, response specifications and full decimated data appear in API exports; the detailed experiment view shows the measured response.

Zero extension at trial boundaries produces edge transients. This is not a stateful real-time decimator; its delay compensation requires future samples. Features and benchmarks continue to use the original acquisition-rate signal to preserve their declared bandwidth. The 1 kHz output's guaranteed passband ends at 400 Hz, not 450 Hz. FIR/IIR comparisons, LMS with a genuine reference signal, explicit DFT comparison and pole-zero plots remain pending.
